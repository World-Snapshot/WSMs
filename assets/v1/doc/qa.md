**How is this work positioned?**

- **WSM is a capability-expansion method.** In our view, this work, like LoRA or ControlNet, is a method for extending existing capabilities. The NLP field is different because the target distributions of its various tasks are highly dense and consistent. Although visual tasks are all called visual tasks, the distributions of their generation targets are, in essence, relatively different. It is therefore highly useful and necessary to rapidly extend RGB-generation methods and use their powerful capabilities to conquer and expand into similar visual tasks. As a unified interface or target, RGB is one of the interfaces through which humans perceive the world, and it is also where the strongest backbones of today's mainstream visual models are found. From any perspective, therefore, developing a method that extends a frozen RGB model is highly significant.

- **Redirection makes capability expansion scalable.** In this setting, the effectiveness and efficiency of expansion are extremely important. Our idea is to redirect a generation process toward the target domain, thereby transforming every task into a generative form---that is, turning image processing into world processing. Under these conditions, even LoRA becomes inadequate when facing thousands of tasks. We must develop a simpler method that expands capabilities more efficiently. This is why we believe that redirection-based generation will be one of the key conceptual shifts for breaking through the difficulty of training computer-vision systems. In summary, redirection-based generation greatly reduces the difficulty of generation and the burden of inference, while the capability-expansion method turns an exponential number of tasks into linear growth.

**How does this method differ from LoRA and ControlNet?**

- **First, the generation mechanism is different.** As discussed in the previous answer, ours is a redirection method. Over the past year, many researchers have applied related ideas to image editing or video editing. Some works have even begun to use titles such as [*Open-Source Image Editing Models Are Zero-Shot Vision Learners*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04566), while another work argues [*Let RGB Be the Language of Vision*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12450). In terms of its mechanism, our method is somewhat like an improved version of [LoRA](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nZeVKeeFYf9): a LoRA that concentrates on redirecting the generation trajectory and learns faster than ordinary LoRA. In our theory, experiments, and tests, we found that a well-pretrained space can be difficult to fine-tune or forcibly retrain through LoRA alone. In a sense, the original output head can even obstruct the change: the pretrained space itself resists being altered. Once a space has been trained well, introducing a new modality becomes difficult, both because it may damage the original space and because the new space itself is difficult to train. These are among the fundamental difficulties of visual tasks.

- **Second, the way capabilities coexist and are activated is different.** Our starting point is that many control-based capability-extension methods, such as [ControlNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhang_Adding_Conditional_Control_to_Text-to-Image_Diffusion_Models_ICCV_2023_paper.html), are good methods, but they overlook what is required if we truly want to use one method for deep expansion. We should create a form of camouflage: every kind of data should be encoded and interpreted through existing knowledge, even when that knowledge cannot genuinely understand it immediately; alternatively, the different kinds of knowledge should at least be kept from conflicting with one another. Consider a person with red--green color blindness and a person with typical color vision. Both can understand an object, but their perceptual knowledge does not need to understand the other person's perception. In the same way, our expansion method creates numerous TunnelingNets. Like opening tunnels, these networks establish different targets and redirect a generation path, while never requiring the two kinds of knowledge to understand one another. They are similar to a combination of LoRA and a [Mixture of Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538): sparsely activating one TunnelingNet is sufficient to redirect the path. This is why, for interpretable capabilities that the backbone has not yet mastered, TunnelingNet learns at least tens of times faster than LoRA.

**What is the frontier of computer vision?**

- **Generation revealed the direction, but not yet the complete solution.** Some readers may have encountered [Vision Banana](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20329) or [Google Veo 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20328) in recent reports. These works show that generative models can generalize to many other visual tasks. We know these third-party works well. Our Overleaf and Git records show that we had noticed similar phenomena approximately ten months before the earlier of these works, Google Veo 3, and our public [Timeline](?page=timeline.md) records the development of these ideas. We do not consider priority here to be the important issue. Many generative models still cannot support large-scale expansion, continued training, linear capability growth, or emergent effects. If they could, a GPT-like moment for vision would already have arrived. We therefore did not rush to publish this work; we continued developing it intensively for approximately one and a half to two years.

- **Editing moved the visual frontier forward.** Roughly six months to one year ago, further advances led some researchers to regard video- and image-editing models as a more forward-looking direction. In an important sense, editing models are indeed especially well suited to pushing the visual frontier further. We fully agree, which is also why the later stage of our work moved in this direction at approximately the same time. By the time LoRA-based enhancement of existing models became popular, we had nearly completed the present system. To ensure that the work was complete, we deliberately postponed its release for approximately four additional months.

- **Redirection is the next step.** The frontier of computer vision has therefore advanced continuously over the past one to two years. The field has not been developing as slowly as is sometimes assumed, and it should not remain at the two-year-old idea that generation itself is vision. That is a correct direction, but it is now time to pay closer attention to editing capabilities and to the more advanced idea of redirection. This progression provides a clearer account of what has actually been happening at the frontier of computer vision.
